I strongly support strict laws to regulate LLMs, because the risks they create are too serious to be left to voluntary self-regulation.

First, LLMs can spread misinformation at scale. Unlike ordinary software, they generate persuasive human-like text instantly and cheaply, which makes them powerful tools for scams, propaganda, fraud, and manipulation. Without strict legal rules, bad actors can use them to deceive millions before anyone can respond.

Second, LLMs can reproduce harmful biases and discrimination. If these systems are used in hiring, education, healthcare, finance, or law, biased outputs can directly harm people’s lives. We should not allow opaque models to make or influence decisions that affect rights and opportunities unless they are strictly tested, audited, and accountable.

Third, the companies building these systems have strong incentives to move fast and release products before they are safe. Profit does not guarantee responsibility. History shows that when technology can cause large-scale harm, laws are necessary to set boundaries and enforce safety standards.

Strict regulation does not mean banning innovation. It means requiring transparency, independent audits, data protection, liability for harm, and clear limits on dangerous uses. That is how we ensure LLMs serve the public good instead of becoming engines of abuse.

In short, when a technology is powerful enough to influence what people believe, buy, and decide, society has a duty to regulate it strictly. Without strong laws, the costs to safety, fairness, and democracy are simply too high.
